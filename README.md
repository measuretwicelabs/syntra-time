# SyntraTime: the phone page

The shop time page for the phone, added to the home screen from Chrome (owner, 17 Nov 2026; Linework `docs/QUOTES_PLAN.md`, section 6b). One timer tracks one task and how long it took; timers do not care what the others are doing; they are not employee hours, and no program prices from them (owner, 20 and 21 Nov 2026). The screens were agreed as mockups: `docs/time_mockups/` (19 Nov 2026) and `docs/time_mockups/07/` (the timer changes of 21 Nov 2026, which stand where the two differ).

The files: `index.html` (the frame and the look), `app.js` (the screens), `syntratime.js` (every sum and rule), `store.js` (what the phone keeps and sends), `view.js` (what the screens show, in order and in words), `help.js` (the Help window's words), `ms.js` (the sign-in and the three calls on the Time folder), `notify.js` (the notifications) and `sw.js` (the service worker, which answers a tap on a notification).

What is built: the sign-in and What happened; the home screen with the open timers, each with Pause and Stop, the phone's own end-of-day check (a switch and a time) and the jobs; what is being timed; the Start screen for every operation, machines too, with one or more workers; one card for a task, with each worker's own Pause; a second timer on the same operation asking first; the CNC as one timer with Cutting and Loading and unloading; Maintenance (the machine, then the kind of work); adding or removing a person or a vehicle; the end-of-day "still going?" box; a trip out, leaving and coming back; one timer opened with its corrections and the long-timer check; time typed in afterwards; a notification for each open timer with Check in; the family's start-up screen; **The wheel** (22 Nov 2026, "SyntraTime 12"): Day, Started, Stopped and How long on Add time after are picked on a scroll wheel (`wheel.js`, one file for the phone and SyntraQuote: a bar that stays, numbers that move under it, one number to a turn of the mouse wheel, a stop at the first and last number); the same in SyntraQuote's Add time after window. **Labels** (owner, 21 Nov 2026, hand test 7: unloading with the labels put on takes longer, so the CNC and Beam saw cutting each ask On or Off on the Start screen, nothing picked, Start waiting; the CNC card has a Labels switch that works only while Loading and unloading runs and the task is not paused, a sheet counting by the switch at the Cutting tap, and Correct this timer corrects it sheet by sheet; Beam saw cutting is set once and never changed, to change it stop the timer and start a new one, and while one runs on a job its screen is the card alone, with no second Start form (22 Nov 2026); a timer from before says "not asked").

- **The source is here**, in `syntraquote/time/`. The page people open lives in the public repository `measuretwicelabs/syntra-time` (GitHub Pages), which is a copy of this folder. Change it here, test it, then copy it there, with a new version line (`VERSION` at the top of `app.js`, shown at the bottom of What happened and on the start-up screen). Copy every file of the folder: since 21 Nov 2026 that includes `notify.js` and `sw.js`, and the service worker must sit beside `index.html` or the notifications have no Check in button.
- **It holds no secret and no data of the owner.** `CLIENT_ID` in `index.html` is the public number of the registration with Microsoft: it is meant to be seen and opens nothing by itself. There is no password, key or "client secret" anywhere, and none is needed: the sign-in is Microsoft's own page, with a code that only the phone that asked can use (PKCE).
- **Where it saves** is given once in the set-up link, after the `#` (so it is sent to no server), and kept on the phone: `https://measuretwicelabs.github.io/syntra-time/#folder=<the Syntra folder inside OneDrive>`. It reads and writes only `<that folder>/Time/`.
- **What it asks Microsoft for:** to read and write the owner's OneDrive files (`Files.ReadWrite`) and to stay signed in (`offline_access`). Nothing else.
- **The registration** (once, by the owner): an app registration named SyntraTime, for personal Microsoft accounts only, with one redirect address of the kind "Single-page application": the page's own address.
- **The logic** is `syntratime.js`: the diaries, the timers, Pause, the CNC's stretches, the checks, the lists, the totals. One file, run on the page, in SyntraQuote and in Node (`tests/node/time.test.js`); its shape is in `docs/DATA.md`, "SyntraTime". The files are version 2 since 21 Nov 2026: a copy of the page made before then refuses them in plain words, and this one refuses the older ones.
- **A new diary since 21 Nov 2026.** The phone keeps the new shape under new names in its own storage and takes a new device id, so it starts a new diary and can never write over an old one. What was tapped before stays in OneDrive, untouched and no longer read.
- **Until SyntraQuote has sent its lists again** (Settings, Shop time, "Send now", once, after it is updated), the phone has the starting lists with no names: a timer is then in the name of whoever is signed in. What happened says once that the old lists could not be used.

## Notifications, and what a phone page can and cannot do

Asked for by the owner on 20 and 21 Nov 2026: one notification for each open timer, gone when the timer stops, ringing every hour, with a Check in button. He asked for one that cannot be removed; told what a page can do, he chose "do whatever we can for now", with a real Android app later.

What it does: after the first timer is started the page asks once, in plain words, and then the phone asks its own question. Each open timer (a trip is one) has a notification: the operation and the job, "Running 1 h 02 min", who, and the last check-in. A paused timer's says "Paused since 2:41 pm" and has no button. **Check in** writes a check-in in the diary: at once when SyntraTime is open; with it closed, the service worker keeps the tap with its moment and the page writes it down when it is next opened. The timer keeps its last check-in, a check-in at or after an end-of-day check answers it, and "Stop at the last check-in" puts a forgotten timer right.

The honest limits:

- **It can be swiped away.** Since Android 14 any notification can be, from a page or from a real app. The page puts it back the next time it looks, about once a minute, but only while the page is open or the phone still keeps it alive behind.
- **The hourly ring is dependable only while SyntraTime is open.** A page that is closed or asleep cannot ring by itself (Chrome's timed notifications were tried and never launched). The ring comes an hour after the start, the last check-in or the last ring, whichever is latest, when the page next looks.
- **With SyntraTime closed, a notification stays as it last was:** the minutes on it do not move, and it does not ring. It is not taken down either, so a timer stopped from another phone leaves its notification up until this page is opened.
- **Check in works with the page closed.**
- If notifications are blocked for the page in the phone's own settings, the page says so under What happened and does not ask again.

**What could not be tested without the real phone:** how Android draws the notification, whether and how it rings, what a swipe does, how long the phone keeps the page alive behind, the phone's own permission question, and whether a notification survives the phone being restarted. The tests show the page putting up, changing and taking down real notifications in a desktop browser, and the service worker answering a tap with the page open and closed.

**Tested by the owner on his own phone, 21 Nov 2026 (hand test 10, every step matched):** turning notifications on from What happened and the phone's own question; one notification for a running timer, arriving without a ring, with Check in; Check in with SyntraTime open; Check in with SyntraTime closed, written down afterwards with the time it was tapped; a paused timer saying "Paused since" with no Check in; the notification gone when the timer is stopped. **Still not tested on the phone:** the ring every hour, what happens after a swipe, and whether a notification survives the phone being restarted.

All with a pretend Microsoft and OneDrive (`tests/ui/time_lib.py`). `tests/README.md` says what each checks.

Not tested: the real sign-in and OneDrive on the real phone, how the fonts look on it, and the notifications there (above). The start-up screen is turned off in the tests by a key in the page's own storage (`syntratime.splash` set to `off`), because it covers the page for four seconds.
